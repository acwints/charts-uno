Make a bar chart online, free

You have a handful of numbers and you need a bar chart: for a slide, a report, a post, or homework. You do not have to open a spreadsheet and fight the chart wizard. Paste your data and Chartsuno draws the chart in the browser.

How it works

  1. Paste your data. Copy a table from a spreadsheet, a doc, or anywhere else. Two columns, labels and values, are enough. Chartsuno reads the table and builds the chart.
  2. Fix anything in the data editor. Change a value, rename a label, or add a row. The chart updates as you edit, so you do not rebuild it.
  3. Tweak it in the chart editor. Set the title, switch to horizontal bars when the names are long, and adjust the look.
  4. Save, export, or share. Previewing does not require an account. Sign in free to keep the chart in My Charts, download a PNG, or copy a share link. A free account includes 10 saved charts a month.

Why not just use a spreadsheet?

Spreadsheets are good at storing numbers. Making a chart that reads well is a separate chore: default colors, cramped labels, and axis settings buried in menus. Chartsuno starts from the chart. You paste data, and editing a number does not throw away the design.

Good uses for a quick bar chart

  • Comparing sales, signups, or survey answers across categories
  • A ranking, such as a top 10, for a post or a newsletter
  • Class or homework assignments that need a labeled graph
  • One slide that sums up results for a meeting

Tips for a bar chart people can read

  • Sort the bars from largest to smallest unless the order means something, like months.
  • Keep labels short. Chartsuno can switch a bar chart to horizontal bars when the category names are long.
  • Use one color unless a color carries meaning.
  • Put the takeaway in the title, for example “Q3 signups doubled”, not only “Signups”.
Is it free?
You can paste data and preview a chart without an account and without paying. A free account saves up to 10 charts a month, and those saved charts can be exported as a PNG or CSV and shared with a link. Paid plans remove that monthly cap. There is no credit card for the free plan.
Do I need to sign up?
Not to paste data and see the chart. Saving to My Charts, downloading a PNG or CSV, and copying a share link need a free account. On the web, Continue with Google creates the account. The iOS app also offers Sign in with Apple.
Can I edit the numbers after the chart is made?
Yes. Open the data editor, change any value or label, and the chart updates. Save the chart if you want the share link and My Charts to show the new numbers.
How do I get the chart into my slides?
Sign in, export a PNG, and drop it into PowerPoint, Keynote, Google Slides, or a doc. You can also copy a share link to the saved chart.
Does it work on my phone?
It runs in the browser, including mobile Safari. An iOS app is in App Review and is not on the App Store yet.
